Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my time in the Computer Science program, I have developed both my technical skills and my understanding of how those skills apply in real-world environments. When I first began the program, my primary focus was on simply getting code to work. Over time, that mindset shifted toward writing cleaner, more efficient, and more maintainable code while also considering the user experience and the overall purpose of the application.

One of the most valuable aspects of this program has been learning how to approach problems from multiple perspectives. In projects such as the Airgead Banking application, I focused on software design and engineering principles, improving code structure, modularity, and input validation. In the Pirate Intelligent Agent project, I applied algorithmic thinking by working with reinforcement learning concepts, where I had to consider how decisions are made based on rewards and how changes to those rewards affect outcomes. In the Grazioso Salvare Dashboard project, I worked with data-driven design, focusing on how information is stored, filtered, and presented to users in a meaningful way.

Collaboration and communication have also been key areas of growth. Although many assignments were completed individually, I developed the ability to communicate technical concepts clearly through written narratives and code reviews. This is especially important in professional environments, where developers must explain their decisions to stakeholders who may not have a technical background. Creating structured and readable code, along with clear documentation, allows others to understand and build upon my work.

Throughout the program, I have strengthened my understanding of data structures and algorithms, learning how they impact the efficiency and performance of applications. I now approach problems with a stronger focus on optimization and scalability rather than just functionality. Additionally, my experience with software engineering practices has improved my ability to design systems that are organized, maintainable, and aligned with industry standards.

Working with databases and data-driven applications has also expanded my skill set. I have learned how to retrieve, filter, and present structured data in ways that support decision making. This includes building applications that allow users to interact with data dynamically, which is an important skill in modern software development.

Another important area of growth has been developing a stronger awareness of security and reliability. I have learned to think more critically about input validation, error handling, and how systems can fail or be misused. This has helped me adopt a more proactive approach to writing secure and dependable code.

Overall, this ePortfolio reflects not only the projects I have completed, but also the progression of my skills and mindset as a computer science student. The artifacts included demonstrate my ability to design and improve software, apply algorithmic thinking, and build data-driven applications. Each project represents a different aspect of my growth and highlights my ability to adapt and improve existing solutions.

As I move forward in my career, I plan to continue building on these skills and applying them to both my professional role as a QA engineer and my long-term goal of developing and growing my own business. This program has provided me with a strong foundation in computer science, and I am confident in my ability to apply what I have learned to real-world challenges.
